The Xiaomi Mi Air Purifier 4 tracks filter life with an RFID counter, not by measuring how dirty the filter actually is. When the counter hits zero, the purifier nags you to buy a new filter, even if the one inside still cleans your air perfectly well. If your air quality readings are still good, you can reset the counter and keep using the same filter.

For older Xiaomi purifier models there are well-documented hacks for this, but the Purifier 4 had none, until a Reddit user worked out the procedure below by trial and error. They report their unit reading under 10 ppm on the old filter, so there was plenty of life left in it. I'm paraphrasing their method here; the original post is linked at the end.

> [!important] This resets the counter, not the filter
> A reset doesn't make the filter any newer. Keep an eye on the air quality your sensor reports. When the readings start getting worse and stay worse, the filter is genuinely spent and needs replacing. No amount of resetting will help at that point.

## The procedure

1. Delete the device from your **Mi Home** app.
2. Unplug the purifier.
3. Press and hold the **display button** on the back of the unit (the one normally used to dim the screen) and keep holding it.
4. While holding the button, plug the purifier back into the outlet.
5. Keep holding until the device **beeps three times**, then release. The unit powers up with the display in black and white.
6. Press the display button again. The front display now shows service text: *WIFI*, *RFID*, numbers, and so on. Press the **power button** (the left button on the front). The reset runs: the letters disappear one by one until only *WIFI* and readings like temperature and humidity remain, and the air-quality color bar flashes through green, yellow, orange and red.
7. Unplug the device, wait a few seconds, plug it back in and turn it on. Filter life is back at **100%**.

Re-add the device in the Mi Home app and you're done.
